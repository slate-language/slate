# slate 0.2.0 on sysl 0.10.1, against the seven yardsticks

The binary is a `bench/pgo.sh` build of dev `b811108` (sysl 0.10.1, gc 0.3.2), the tree tagged as 0.2.0
apart from the version number. `bench/check.sh` first: every twin's answers unchanged. Then
`bench/run.sh -n 5`, best of five wall times in milliseconds, run as one 12-unit marshal job (the whole
budget). The machine was not quiet: the 15-minute load average was 21 shortly before the run, and work
outside marshal kept running. Best-of-five absorbs most of that; read the ratios as good to about 0.1x.

## Geometric means, slate's time over theirs

| binary | lua | node --jitless | python3 | qjs | ruby | php | luajit -joff |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1.32, sysl 0.8.8 (`2026-10-30-sysl-0-8-8.md`) | 1.0x | 0.7x | 0.5x | 0.6x | 0.5x | 0.7x | 1.3x |
| **0.2.0, sysl 0.10.1** | **0.8x** | **0.6x** | **0.4x** | **0.5x** | **0.4x** | **0.6x** | **1.1x** |

slate is now ahead of PUC-Rio Lua on the geometric mean, and within 10% of LuaJIT's interpreter.

## `fib`, which does not depend on the load

`bench/fib.sl` under `/usr/bin/time -l`:

| release | instructions | cycles |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.1.18 | 5.06G | 634M |
| 0.1.32 | 6.31G | 1017M |
| 0.2.0 | 6.16G | 861M |

Instructions fell 2% from 0.1.32 and cycles 15%, so most of the gain is fewer stalls rather than less
work. The gap to 0.1.18's instruction count (`2026-11-11-the-gap-to-0-1-18.md`) is still there.
