# 2026-09-22 — THE FOUR FUSED ARMS GO BACK OUT OF LINE, AND THE COLD-ARM MOVE STAYS — profile 4's item 1

**[The fourth sampled profile](2026-09-22-sampled-profile-4.md) measured `inline-fused` (`24a2cb0`) at
**+1.19%** of the geometric mean against sysl 0.0.125, where it had measured -1.96% against 0.0.123.**
That commit did two things at once — it wrote the four superinstruction arms out inside `run_frames`,
and it moved seven cold arms out to `run_compose.sysl` to make room — and only the pair was ever
timed together. This page times them apart.

**Three trees, one worktree, one compiler, three binaries interleaved:**

| | what it is | `run_frames.sysl` |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | dev `4f85d2b` as it stands | 971 lines |
| **V** | the four fused arms back OUT of line, the seven cold arms LEFT in `run_compose.sysl` | 941 lines |
| **R** | the full revert of `24a2cb0`'s four source files | 994 lines |

**V is what landed.** Both undo the loss; **V -0.70% and R -0.78% over profile 4's twenty**, and the
0.09% between them is smaller than the gap between two runs of the same pair. V keeps the hottest
file fifty lines shorter and keeps `compose_unpack_slots` and `Buf.set<Value>` visible under their own
names in a sampled profile, which is what the cold-arm move bought and is worth keeping for nothing.

### How it was measured

**One worktree at three states, six builds, one compiler (sysl 0.0.125).** Each state's four files
were copied in, `sysl build .` run for the timing binary and `sysl build . --features profile` for the
counting one, so nothing about the three differs except those four files. Timed with
`bench/timeit.pl` under `caffeinate -dimsu` on a box at 91.2% idle with no JVM running, **alternating
best-of-9 over all three binaries at once** — H, V and R back to back on each program, nine rounds,
the lowest of each kept. The whole thing was then run a second time from the same binaries, which is
what makes the V-against-R answer readable at all.

### V against R, which is the cold-arm move priced on its own

**The two differ in nothing but where the seven cold arms are written**, so the column above is the
whole of what that half of `24a2cb0` is worth — and the answer is **nothing either way**. It is not
that the difference is small everywhere: `branches` prefers R by about 1.4 points in both runs and
`nested` prefers V by about one, each reproducibly. They cancel. **Profile 4's open question — whether
the cold-arm move was carrying part of the −1.19% rather than fighting it — is answered: it was doing
neither**, and the reason it can be kept is that it costs nothing rather than that it pays.

So the tiebreak is the ordinary one. `run_frames.sysl` is the file the thousand-line rule is tightest
on; R puts it back to 994 lines with six to spare, and V leaves it at 941.

### The disassembly, and why nothing says `@noinline`

`nm -n` over the three binaries, and `otool -tvV` for the call sites:

| | H (dev) | V (landed) | R |
|---|---|---|---|
| the four `fused_*` symbols | **not in the binary** | all four present | all four present |
| `bl` sites naming each of them | — | **exactly one each** | one each |
| `run_frames` | 40,300 bytes | **38,988 bytes** | 49,232 bytes |
| the out-of-line bodies beside it | — | 2,048 bytes (the four plus `stored_in_slot`) | the same four |

**`-O2` does not re-absorb them and cannot, so the attribute would be decoration.** `private` in sysl
is private to the FILE: a body called from `run_frames.sysl` and written in `run_fused.sysl` cannot be
`private`, and a module-visible name lowers to an LLVM `define` with external linkage whose body must
be kept whatever the inliner decides — so the single-caller bonus that would bring one under the
threshold never applies. That is `2026-09-21-inline-fused.md`'s own finding read backwards: **the file
split is what keeps a body out of line, and moving it into its caller's file is what inlines it.**
`@noinline` (sysl 0.0.123+) is therefore held in reserve for the day one of these is written in
`run_frames.sysl` for some other reason; as long as it lives here the linkage decides.

**R's `run_frames` is 49,232 bytes against V's 38,988 and is not measurably slower for it**, which is
the same thing profile 4 said from the other end: the loop that is 18% *smaller* was the slow one, so
size is not what this function is short of.

### The instruction counts, which had to be identical and are

`sysl build . --features profile` for each of the three, run over `arith`, `branches`, `loops`, `fib`
and `nested`: **the `instruction kinds executed` tables are identical line for line across H, V and
R**, as are the totals — `arith` 130,000,025, `branches` 121,451,942, `fib` 119,760,624, `nested`
84,108,048, `loops` 64,138,039. The reports differ only in the microsecond figures beside a builtin's
call count, which are clocks. This is a codegen-shape change and the machine executes exactly what it
executed, which is what rule 4-0 rests on; `tests_fused.sysl` still pins every fused pair as
answer-for-answer identical to the two instructions it replaces.

### What is left of profile 4's item 1

**Struck.** The loss is off, the mean is back where it was before `inline-fused` landed under sysl
0.0.125, and the fused pairs are calls again with a paragraph in `run_fused.sysl` saying why — so the
next session that reads "these four are `bl`s, they should be arms" finds the measurement before it
makes the change. `Buf.push` stays inlined in sysl 0.0.124: it is the half of the pair that is free,
and the two were only ever competing for `run_frames`' registers.
