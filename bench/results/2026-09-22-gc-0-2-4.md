# 2026-09-22 — `sh.sysl.gc` 0.2.4: the allocator stops walking the bin array

**This is the fix the `gc-alloc` investigation proposed, built in the package and picked up here.**
That page named `take_larger`'s linear scan of the 88 size classes as essentially all of `gc.alloc`'s
6.23% weighted self time — up to 87 dependent loads per allocation, and the steady state rather than a
warm-up, because `rebuild_free` coalesces dead cells into large blocks and `split` returns the
remainder to a large class, so slate's exact small class is usually empty. gc 0.2.4 (tag `2f3e52e`)
gives `struct Heap` a two-word bitmap of the non-empty classes and reads it with `trailing_zeros`, so
the search for the next non-empty bin is a shift, a compare and a count. The package's own micro-bench
reports **2.35x** on allocate-and-drop.

**Nothing under `dev/` moved: `package.hocon`'s `gc` version and `sysl.sum`'s new line are the whole
diff.** Under rule 4-0 this owes no new test — it is a dependency bump with no behaviour of slate's
own to pin, and `bench/check.sh` reports every one of the twenty-three programs answering exactly what
it answered before, on all four implementations.

**Measured: geometric mean −2.46% on an alternating best-of-9, with the three allocation-dense
programs taking most of it — `alloc` −18.8%, `calls` −15.5%, `csv` −11.9%.**

**The seven programs reading +1.2 to +1.8% allocate almost nothing**, and the change cannot reach
them: the branch's `alloc` is the same function with a different bin search, so a program that rarely
calls it has nothing to gain and is reading the ordinary between-build band. What separates signal
from band here is that the three programs the investigation *named* are the three that moved, in the
order it predicted, and by ten times the band.

**Against the predicted ceiling: the per-program half came out at about two thirds of it and the mean
at about 40%.** `gc-alloc` said 20 to 28% of `alloc`, `calls` and `csv` each, and about 6% of the mean.
What arrived is 18.8 / 15.5 / 11.9% and 2.46%. The gap is the part of `gc.alloc` that was never the
scan — the header write, the object-list link, the counters and the `_bzero` every constructor
immediately writes over — plus the bit scan's own cost, which is small but not zero. The witness below
prices what is left.

## The witness: `gc$alloc`'s self time on `alloc.sl`

`/usr/bin/sample` at 1 ms, three runs of `bench/alloc.sl` per side, self time from "Sort by top of
stack, same collapsed" against the main thread's own sample total. Box at 92.3% idle, `pgrep -x java`
empty, under `caffeinate -dimsu`.

| | control (gc 0.2.3) | branch (gc 0.2.4) |
|---|---|---|
| samples (main thread) | 443 / 491 / 481 = **1,415** | 380 / 369 / 378 = **1,127** |
| `gc$alloc` self time | 118 / 118 / 130 = **366** | 42 / 27 / 41 = **110** |
| share | **25.9%** | **9.8%** |

**`gc$alloc` goes from the top of the program's profile to fourth**, under `run_frames`,
`_platform_memset` and `gc$collect` — and the whole program's sample total falls with it, which is the
same 18.8% the clock reports arriving by another route. What is left is the part that was never the
scan: the investigation put the header write, the list link and the counters at 10 of 96 samples on
this program, and `_bzero`/`_platform_memset` beside them.

## Instruction counts, and the one counter that cannot see this

**Identical to the instruction on all three programs**, from a `--features profile` build of each
worktree — `alloc` **57,000,025**, `calls` **48,000,046**, `csv` **14,340,921** — which is what a
change wholly inside the allocator has to read. `collections` (3,690), `heap in use` (116,020 bytes)
and `heap high water` (997,396 bytes) are identical too, so the collector is being asked for the same
work at the same moments and only the search in front of it changed.

**`alloc_steps` is identical as well — 8,997,559 — and that is not a null result, it is the counter
measuring something else.** The `gc-alloc` proposal wondered whether `h.steps` belonged behind a
feature, being a read-modify-write inside the scan. Reading both versions settles it: 0.2.3 charges a
step per block *examined* in `take_from_bin` and one per successful `take_larger` hit, never one per
bin walked, so the 82-iteration walk was invisible to it all along. 0.2.4 charges at the same two
places. **So the diagnostic could never have witnessed this change**, the wall clock and the sampler
are the whole of the evidence, and nobody should read a flat `alloc_steps` as the bitmap not working.

## What this closes

**The `gc.alloc` line comes off the ranked shortlist as DONE**, struck in
[profile 4](2026-09-22-sampled-profile-4.md) with this page's numbers. It was the largest single item
any of the four profiles had named and the only one that was entirely somebody else's package;
slate's own half of it — one `current()` per allocation instead of three — landed at
[`gc-alloc`](2026-09-22-gc-alloc.md) on 2026-09-22 for −0.62% / −0.28%.

**What remains inside `gc.alloc` is a format question rather than a search question**, and the
investigation already named it and declined to propose it: the 40-byte `Header` in front of a 40-byte
`StrObj` is 50% overhead on slate's commonest cell, inflating `need`, the class it lands in and the
`_bzero`. That reaches the sweep, `rebuild_free` and every offset, so it is the package author's
decision and not a pickup. An `alloc_raw` that skips the zeroing is the same kind of question.

**A fresh sampled profile is owed before the next item is chosen.** This is the second change in a day
to land underneath the fourth profile — `lookup-hit-path`, `match-leaf`, `module-cells` and `unfuse`
are the others — so the live ranking of what is left is not known with confidence from any page
currently here.
