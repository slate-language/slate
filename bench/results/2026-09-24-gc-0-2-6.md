# 2026-09-24 — `sh.sysl.gc` 0.2.6: a program with no weak value walks no weak object

**gc 0.2.6 skips a collection's ephemeron pass and its weaken pass where no live object has the hook,
and on slate that was worth nothing until the two reserves stopped being weak.** The package keeps two
counts on the `Heap` — live objects whose kind carries a `weaken` hook and those carrying an
`ephemeron` hook — and exposes them as `gc.weak_objects(h)` and `gc.ephemeron_objects(h)`, beside
`gc.weak_walks(h)`, which counts the passes it did run. But `reserve_spares` in `obj.sysl` took
`spare_weak_map` under `weak_map_kind` and `spare_weak_ref` under `weak_ref_kind`, and `mark_spares`
keeps both alive for the whole run, so both counts were always at least one and every collection of
every program walked the weak objects.

**The reserves are strong now**: the weak map's under `object_kind`, the weak reference's under a new
`held_ref_kind` (`trace_held_ref` marks the one `Value`; no finalizer, no weak hook). With that and the
pin moved to 0.2.6: **`csv` −13.4%, `alloc` −9.8%, `strings` −7.9%, geometric mean −1.68%**, and `csv`'s
collector time **−31%**.

## Why a strong reserve is right, and why not a lazy one

A reserve is handed out only when the heap is full, and the statement that took it is the program's
last — the statement boundary reports the out-of-memory against its line. A weak map that never drops
an entry, or a weak reference that never clears, is exactly as good for that one statement. And the
strong direction is the SAFE one: holding what it was given can only keep something alive, never
leave a slot naming freed storage. Each is still traced and finalized as the storage it is —
`object_kind` marks both halves of the table and `finalize_object` releases its `Buf`s exactly as
`weak_map_kind` did; `held_ref_kind` marks the target of a `WeakRefObj`, which owns nothing to release.

**Taking the reserve lazily under the real kind was considered and not done.** The moment a reserve
is needed is the moment the heap is full, so it cannot be allocated then; the lazy form would have to
take it at the first `WeakMap()` instead, and that call can itself meet a full heap — so a fallback
reserve is needed anyway, plus a branch on every `new_weak_map`. The strong reserve is one line per
kind and no branch anywhere.

## The witness

`tests_weak.sysl`, at the sysl level where a collection can be forced:

- `A_HEAP_WITH_ONLY_ITS_RESERVES_HOLDS_NO_WEAK_OBJECT` — after `reset_heap`, `gc.weak_objects` and
  `gc.ephemeron_objects` are 0 and a collection leaves `gc.weak_walks` at 0.
- `A_PROGRAM_WITH_NO_WEAK_VALUE_COLLECTS_WITHOUT_EVER_WALKING_THE_WEAK_OBJECTS` — a program that
  collects more than five times (asserted, as the control) ends with `weak_walks` at 0.
- `A_PROGRAM_HOLDING_A_WEAK_MAP_IS_WALKED_AT_EVERY_COLLECTION` — the same program with one `WeakMap`
  held: one weak object, at least one walk per collection, and the held key keeps its entry.
- `A_FULL_HEAP_ANSWERS_A_WEAK_MAP_WITH_ITS_RESERVE_AND_THE_RESERVE_HOLDS_WHAT_IT_WAS_GIVEN` — on a
  full 256 KiB heap `new_weak_map` answers `spare_weak_map`, and an entry whose key and value nothing
  else holds survives a collection and reads back (a weak table would have dropped it).
- `A_WEAK_MAP_MADE_ON_A_FULL_HEAP_GETS_THE_ORDINARY_OUT_OF_MEMORY_SENTENCE` — the error path a program
  sees, against the statement that was running.

The existing `A_FULL_HEAP_ANSWERS_A_WEAK_REFERENCE_WITH_ITS_RESERVE_AND_reset_heap_TAKES_ANOTHER`
covers the re-kinded weak reference reserve unchanged.
