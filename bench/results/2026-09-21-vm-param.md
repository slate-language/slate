# 2026-09-21 — THE VM IS HANDED DOWN INSTEAD OF BEING ASKED FOR AGAIN — shortlist item 3

**`current()` compiles to a thread-local lookup, and on this platform that is a `_tlv_get_addr` CALL
into `libdyld` that no optimiser removes.** The
[second sampled profile](2026-09-21-sampled-profile-2.md) put it at **4.28% of weighted wall time**
across the set and named its hot callers: `run_frames`, `maybe_collect`, `keepable`, `pop` and
`fused_less_jump_if_false`. `run_frames` already asked once and kept the answer; every helper it
reached asked again, once per instruction.

**Measured: -4.06% on the alternating best-of-9 geometric mean**, against a ceiling of 3–4%. Control
is dev `3b5026c`.

## What changed

Five helpers grew a form that takes what the loop is already holding, and the loop hands it down.

| the helper | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| `pop` / `peek` (`execute.sysl`) | `machine()` per call | `pop_on(vm)` / `peek_on(vm, back)` take the `*Machine`; the old spellings remain as one-line wrappers for cold callers |
| the fused arms (`run_fused.sysl`) | `fused_less_jump_if_false(at)` popped through `machine()` twice | takes the `*Machine`, as its three siblings already did |
| `keepable` (`runtime.sysl`) | `failure(at, …)` reads `current().current_source` | `keepable_on(vm, …)` takes the `*Vm`; `keepable` is the wrapper |
| `maybe_collect` (`obj.sysl`) | `current()` and `slate_heap()` | `maybe_collect_on(v)`, and there is no wrapper because the safe point has exactly one caller |
| `run_frames`' own `PushModule`, `LoadDef` and the `Tick` arm | `current().…` | `here.…`, the pointer the loop already took |

**The mechanical part is `run_frames.sysl`**: 53 `pop()` and 28 `peek(n)` sites become `pop_on(vm)`
and `peek_on(vm, n)`, and 11 `keepable(…)` become `keepable_on(here, …)`. The file did not grow —
every edit is a replacement, and the one comment that was rewritten replaced another.

**The cold callers keep the form they had**, which is the whole reason the wrappers exist: `apply`,
the builtin callback path, the spread call and everything in `run_compose.sysl` (documented there as
the arms that are cold) were left exactly as they were. Only what the samples named was changed.

## Why a cached pointer is safe here, case by case

**`current()` changes for exactly two reasons and neither of them happens under a running
`run_frames`.**

- **`enter`/`leave` swap this thread's `the_vm`**, and the runtime itself never calls them — a test or
  an embedder does, around a whole `run`. There is no path from an instruction to either.
- **`the_vm` is `@thread_local`**, so an actor's second VM lives on its own thread with its own
  `run_frames` and its own `here`. Two VMs cannot see each other's pointer at all.

**A park does NOT invalidate the machine pointer, and that is what makes `pop_on(vm)` correct across a
generator.** `vm` is `&current().machine` — the address of a FIELD of the VM, not of a machine
object. `park()` moves the running machine into `parked` and installs a fresh one *in that field*, and
`unpark` puts it back; the address is the same throughout. So a nested line of execution — a
generator stepped by `gen_step`, a coroutine, a builtin running a slate callback — leaves the outer
loop's `vm` pointing at the outer loop's machine when control comes back. **That guarantee is not new:
`run_frames` has held this pointer across all 116 of its `vm.stack`/`vm.frames` sites since the VM
work landed, and every arm after a call reads through it.** What this item does is extend the same
cached pointer to `pop`, `peek` and the fused arms, which were asking again for an answer the loop had.

**`here` is a `*Vm` and the fields read through it are the VM's own** — `module_values`, `defs`,
`heap_exhausted`, `current_source` — none of which a park touches. `maybe_collect_on(here)` reads
`here.heap` for the same reason `slate_heap()` did.

**`keepable_on` is the one that needed a second look**, because the sentence it builds names the file
a span belongs to and that field MOVES: `run_frames` writes `here.current_source` at entry and at
every point that changes `chunk_ix`. A nested run sets it and puts it back. Reading it through `here`
at the moment of the refusal is therefore exactly what `failure` was doing through `current()` —
`AN_ABSENCE_REFUSED_INSIDE_A_CALLBACK_QUOTES_THE_LINE_IT_WAS_WRITTEN_ON` in `tests_fused.sysl` is the
test that says so, refusing an absence inside a `map` callback and asserting the quoted line.

## The witness: `_tlv_get_addr` on `fib`

`/usr/bin/sample` at a 1 ms interval, the program started and sampled in one shell call, under
`caffeinate` on the same quiet box — the second sampled profile's own method. The figure is
`_tlv_get_addr`'s self time out of the main thread's sample total from the call graph's head.

| | samples of `_tlv_get_addr` | main-thread total | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| control (`3b5026c`) | 25 | 573 | **4.4%** |
| branch | 2 | 541 | **0.4%** |

**On the branch it falls below `sample`'s own five-sample cut-off**, so it no longer appears in the
self-time table at all; the two remaining samples are in the call graph, reached from the start-up
path rather than from the loop. What is left in the hot path of `fib` is `run_frames` (213 samples),
`Buf.push<Value>` (100) and `placed_call` (36) — the three the profile already ranks above this item.

**The named callers moved with it**: `keepable`/`keepable_on` 17 → 20 samples and
`maybe_collect`/`maybe_collect_on` 18 → 17 out of a smaller total, which is the lookup coming out of
them and the work they actually do staying where it was.

## The tests

The behaviour is invisible from a program, so these pin the paths that were changed rather than an
answer that moved.

- **`tests/lang/fused.sl`** — `A_GENERATOR_STEPPED_FROM_A_FOLDED_LOOP_ANSWERS_EVERY_VALUE_IN_ORDER`
  and `TWO_GENERATORS_INTERLEAVED_IN_ONE_FOLDED_LOOP_KEEP_THEIR_OWN_COUNTERS`. Two operand stacks with
  fused instructions on both sides of every crossing; a value taken off the wrong one is a wrong sum
  and a repeated number, which is what the two assertions are.
- **`tests/lang/actors.sl`** —
  `an_actor_and_the_program_run_the_same_loop_and_each_keeps_its_own_count`. Two VMs on two threads
  running the same arithmetic at once, asserted apart.
- **`tests_fused.sysl`**, which is where the crowded-heap versions live, `tests_native_args.sysl`'s
  arrangement: `A_GENERATOR_STEPPED_FROM_A_FOLDED_LOOP_ANSWERS_WITH_THE_COLLECTOR_RUNNING_UNDER_IT`
  (300 crossings against a megabyte heap), `TWO_GENERATORS_INTERLEAVED_UNDER_A_COLLECTION_KEEP_THEIR_OWN_STACKS`,
  `A_BUILTIN_RUNNING_A_CALLBACK_INSIDE_A_FOLDED_LOOP_LEAVES_THE_STACK_WHERE_IT_WAS` (ten thousand
  turns, so a one-cell drift is arithmetic rather than luck) and
  `AN_ABSENCE_REFUSED_INSIDE_A_CALLBACK_QUOTES_THE_LINE_IT_WAS_WRITTEN_ON`.

## What is left of the item

**Nothing on the hot path that the samples can see.** `gc.alloc`'s `slate_heap()` is still a lookup
per allocation and is counted under allocation rather than here; threading the VM through every
allocation site reaches forty-two call sites in `obj.sysl` alone and belongs with whatever eventually
answers the profile's open question about what `calls` and `csv` allocate per call.
